# Hector — The Offering

The answer to "what exactly are we offering?", written down so we can hold
ourselves to it. The interactive demo (`demo/index.html`) is this document
made clickable.

## The one-sentence offer

**Pay £149–£249 a month, spend ten minutes a month, and walk into report
season with the work already done.**

Hector is not "compliance software." It is the absorption of a specific,
hated, recurring job: keeping the evidence and writing the report.

## How it changes their life (before → after)

| | Before Hector | With Hector |
|---|---|---|
| All year | Evidence scattered across inbox, drawers, CRM, memory | One vault; one 10-minute prompt a month files everything |
| FCA publishes something | Skim the trade press, hope it doesn't apply | Next morning: "Here's what changed, here's the one thing you must do" |
| Report season | A fortnight of dread, a blank Word document, weekends lost | An afternoon: read the draft, click the footnotes, approve |
| Cost | £2,500–£10,000/yr consultancy that still leaves the writing to them | £1,788–£2,988/yr, writing included |
| The brown envelope arrives | Panic assembly of evidence under deadline | "Here's the file" — same day |

The emotional product is **the end of low-grade year-round guilt**. Small
principals know they're behind on compliance admin even when they're good
advisers. Hector sells a clear conscience on a subscription.

## The trust architecture

An AI compliance product must earn trust structurally, not rhetorically:

1. **Show the source.** Every sentence in every generated report carries a
   clickable citation to a real document in the firm's vault. Nothing is
   asserted that can't be traced. This is the single most important
   product decision.
2. **Flag gaps, never paper over them.** When evidence is missing, Hector
   says so in the draft, visibly, with a suggested action. An AI that
   admits what it doesn't know is the only kind a compliance buyer can
   trust — and it converts a trust problem into a to-do list.
3. **The human approves.** Nothing leaves Hector without the SMF holder
   reviewing and approving it. Hector drafts; the principal owns. (Also
   the correct regulatory position: responsibility cannot be delegated.)
4. **Practise what we preach.** UK hosting, no training on client data,
   full access audit trail, export/delete anytime — stated plainly, and
   our own security documentation published. A compliance product with
   sloppy compliance is dead on arrival.
5. **Aligned pricing.** The price-lock promise (no mid-term rises, CPI cap
   at renewal, in the contract) signals we make money by keeping firms,
   not by trapping them.
6. **Later, borrowed trust:** partnerships with compliance consultancies
   ("Hector keeps the file, your consultant reviews it"), trade-body
   endorsements, professional indemnity insurance, ICO registration shown
   in the footer.

## How self-serve can it be?

Fully, by design — card to value without speaking to anyone:

- **Minute 0–5:** readiness score (already built) → sees their gaps
- **Minute 5–10:** card details, firm name, FRN
- **Minute 10–50:** guided intake; uploads whatever exists (Hector reads
  it; messy is fine — messy is the point)
- **Day 1:** working vault, first gap list, first month's prompt scheduled
- **Month 1–11:** ten minutes a month
- **Month 12:** the afternoon

The only human touchpoints: email support (Solo), one optional onboarding
call (Firm). Anything that *requires* a human conversation to onboard is a
product defect to be fixed, not a service tier to be sold.

## Unit economics (why few customers is enough)

- Inference + hosting per customer: low single-digit pounds/month at this
  workload (document ingestion at onboarding, then light monthly use and
  one heavy drafting burst a year) → **~95%+ gross margin**.
- 100 customers at blended ~£200/mo ≈ **£240k ARR**, operable by one
  founder with AI doing support triage, content and the product itself.
- 500 customers ≈ £1.2M ARR — still ~1% of the 5,500 advice firms, before
  brokers, mortgage intermediaries or networks.

## The replication thesis

Hector v1 is FCA/Consumer Duty, but the engine is general:

> **regulator watcher + evidence vault + monthly prompt + cited report
> generator**

Each new market is a new regulatory pack on the same engine: CQC for care
providers (~30k locations), Awaab's Law for social landlords, Cyber
Essentials, then other jurisdictions (CBI in Ireland, MAS in Singapore,
ASIC in Australia — all have small-firm conduct regimes). The brand
pattern repeats: a human name, a niche regulator, a price the incumbents
abandoned. Prove the model once, then replicate deliberately.
