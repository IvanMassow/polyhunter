READ THIS FIRST — CONTEXT FOR THE AUDIT DATA
================================================

You are looking at a structured trading audit dataset from a system
called PolyHunter. This document tells you what you are looking at,
how the system works, and what every field means. Read this before
touching the CSVs.


WHAT THIS IS
------------
PolyHunter is a paper-trading system that bets on prediction markets
(Polymarket). Contracts trade between 0.00 and 1.00, where the price
represents the market's implied probability of a real-world event
happening. We buy contracts where we believe the market has the
probability wrong.

This is NOT live trading. All positions are simulated with virtual
capital ($140,000 total across 62 candidates).

The dataset covers 62 prediction market candidates tracked over an
8-day observation window as of 2026-02-20.


WHAT WE BUY AND WHY
--------------------
We buy mispriced probability. The edge is the gap between what we
think the probability is and what the market is pricing.

Direction tells you which side we are on:
  HIGHER = we bought YES (we think price goes up, event more likely)
  LOWER  = we bought NO  (we think price goes down, event less likely)

All prices are decimals. 0.67 = 67 cents = 67% implied probability.


HOW WE FIND EDGES
-----------------
Edges come from narrative analysis — real-world signals (news, policy
shifts, geopolitical events) that the prediction market hasn't priced
in yet. Three categories:

  CONSENSUS  = broad agreement supports this view, market is just slow
  DISCOVERY  = we spotted something the market hasn't noticed yet
  FRONTIER   = speculative, thin evidence, but cheap entry


HOW WE RATE CANDIDATES
-----------------------
Every candidate gets a letter rating:

  A = BLUE CHIP     — highest confidence, act on these (21 candidates)
  B = BREAD & BUTTER — moderate confidence, standard trades (21 candidates)
  C = CONTESTED     — mixed signals, evidence vs counter-evidence (7 candidates)
  D = DARK HORSE    — emerging signal, interesting asymmetry (11 candidates)
  E = FRONTIER SCOUT — thin evidence, cheap, speculative (2 candidates)

Edge strength is also scored numerically as "edge_points" (4 to 15).
Higher = stronger predicted edge. This is independent of the letter.


HOW WE SIZE POSITIONS
---------------------
Three tiers (the labels are counterintuitive):

  HIGH risk   = $2,000  per position  (uncertain outcome, small bet)
  MEDIUM risk = $5,000  per position
  LOW risk    = $10,000 per position  (high conviction, larger bet)

LOW risk gets the biggest size because the edge is most confident.


HOW WE ENTER
------------
We do NOT buy immediately. A candidate starts in WATCHING state.

Entry rule: buy on the FIRST UPTICK after the edge is identified.
This confirms the market is starting to move our way before we
commit capital. Until that uptick happens, the position stays
WATCHING and no trade is recorded.

In the timeline data, entry is marked with a "B" (Buy) marker.


HOW WE EXIT
-----------
Five mechanical exit rules, applied in order of priority:

  1. TRAILING STOP — exit if price retraces 0.5 cents from peak
  2. PLATEAU       — exit if no price improvement for 4 hours
  3. MAX HOLD      — hard exit after 12 hours regardless
  4. TARGET        — exit when specific price target is hit
  5. TOKEN CORRECTION — exit on minor price correction

No discretionary exits. These rules are applied mechanically.


HOW WE KILL POSITIONS
---------------------
A KILL is different from an exit. Kills happen when the thesis breaks:

  SENTIMENT REVERSED  — the fundamental reason for the trade flipped
  DUPLICATE SLUG      — same event tracked twice, duplicate removed
  SPREAD EATS EDGE    — entry spread >= 4c, friction kills the edge


RE-ENTRIES
----------
If we exit a position and the edge later re-emerges on the same
event, we can re-enter. In the data:
  - Same event_id, but new signal_id and new trade_id
  - Appears as separate rows — do NOT merge them
  - Each instance has its own entry price, exit, and P&L

Candidates cluster into macro themes. The system tracks concentration:

  Fed Rate Cuts   — HIGH exposure  (11 positions)
  Inflation       — MEDIUM exposure (4 positions)
  Trump Tariffs   — MEDIUM exposure (4 positions)
  Geopolitical/Iran — multiple positions
  Fed Personnel   — 2 positions
  Commodities     — 2-3 positions
  Real Estate     — 1 position


HOLD DURATION BACKTESTING
--------------------------
The system tracks what would have happened if positions were held
longer than the 12h max hold rule:

  Hold  1h  →  +0.6% avg,  29% win rate
  Hold  6h  →  +4.3% avg,  54% win rate
  Hold 12h  →  +8.1% avg,  74% win rate  ← current rule
  Hold 24h  → +12.9% avg,  79% win rate
  Hold 48h  → +133.4% avg, 83% win rate  (6 trades only, skewed)

This suggests the 12h cap may be leaving money on the table, but
the 48h number is from a tiny sample with one +640% outlier.


WHAT THE CSV FILES CONTAIN
---------------------------
  SIGNAL_LOG.csv             — every signal change with UTC timestamps
  PRICE_SERIES.csv           — hourly price observations per event
  EXECUTED_TRADES_HISTORY.csv — entry/exit for every trade leg
  POSITION_STATE_OVER_TIME.csv — hourly snapshots of position state
  SUMMARY_STATS_PER_EVENT.csv — aggregate stats per event
  STATIC_POSITIONS_FROM_HTML.csv — full detail on all 62 positions


DATA LIMITATIONS YOU NEED TO KNOW
----------------------------------
  - Only 21 of 62 events have hourly time-series data (from git).
    The other 41 have current-state snapshots only.
  - No bid/ask data. Only last_traded_price. Those columns are blank.
  - No volume or volatility per timestamp. Blank where unavailable.
  - Time series covers ~21 hours. Positions may predate this window.
  - Hourly granularity at best. No tick-level data.
  - Blank fields mean data unavailable. Nothing has been interpolated.
