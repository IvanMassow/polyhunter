POLYHUNTER TRADING AUDIT — STRATEGY CONTEXT
=============================================
This document describes what the PolyHunter system is, how it trades,
and what rules govern its behaviour. It is intended to accompany the
CSV audit sheets so that any downstream model or analyst loading them
cold has full context for what they are looking at.

No optimisation. No interpretation. Just a factual description of the
system as it operates.


1. WHAT IS POLYHUNTER
---------------------
PolyHunter is a prediction-market edge tracker. It is part of the NOAH
system (Narrative Signal tracking suite). It monitors Polymarket — a
platform where contracts trade between 0 and 1.00, representing the
probability of a real-world event occurring.

The system identifies narrative-driven edges in prediction markets,
tracks them as candidates, and paper-trades them using mechanical
rules. No real capital is deployed. All positions are simulated.

As of 2026-02-20:
  - 62 long-tail candidates tracked (8-day observation window)
  - 19 winners, 6 losers (76% win rate on crystallised trades)
  - $140,000 virtual capital deployed across all positions
  - +$11,384 total dollar P&L (+8.1% portfolio return)
  - Best trade: +44.3%, Worst trade: -52.3%


2. WHAT WE ARE BUYING
----------------------
Polymarket contracts. Each contract prices between 0.00 and 1.00 (or
equivalently 0c to 100c). The price represents the market's implied
probability of an event occurring.

We are buying contracts where we believe the market misprices the
probability. The "edge" is the gap between our assessed probability
and the market price.

Direction:
  - HIGHER = we are buying YES contracts (we think the event is more
    likely than the market implies, so the price should go up)
  - LOWER = we are buying NO contracts (we think the event is less
    likely than the market implies, so the price should go down)

All prices in the CSVs are decimals: 0.67 means 67 cents, representing
a 67% implied probability.


3. EDGE DISCOVERY THEORY
--------------------------
The system identifies edges through narrative analysis. An edge exists
when a real-world narrative signal (news, policy, geopolitics) creates
a probability shift that the prediction market has not yet priced in.

Three edge types:

  CONSENSUS  — Broad market agreement supports this view but the
               contract is mispriced. Highest confidence. Most common.
  DISCOVERY  — A novel or underrecognised signal that the market has
               not yet incorporated. Tracked with a separate "discovery
               price" (marked as "d:XX.Xc" in the data).
  FRONTIER   — Emerging/speculative signal with thin evidence.
               Asymmetric payoff but low conviction.

Edge strength is scored as "edge points" (range: 4 to 15). Higher =
stronger predicted edge. This is NOT the same as the rating.

      Average confirmation time: 24h.

  B — BREAD & BUTTER
      Moderate confidence, standard opportunities.
      21 candidates. 50.0% win rate. -0.2% avg P&L.
      Average confirmation time: 12h.

  C — CONTESTED
      Evidence vs counter-signals. Mixed conviction.
      7 candidates. 0% win rate (small sample).
      Average confirmation time: 21h.

  D — DARK HORSE
      Emerging signal, interesting asymmetry. Lower data.
      11 candidates.

  E — FRONTIER SCOUT
      Thin evidence, cheap entry, speculative.
      2 candidates.

Some positions have a "system rating" that differs from the "report
rating" (shown as a subscript in the HTML, e.g., A with subscript B).
The report rating is what governs the position; the system rating
reflects an automated assessment.


5. POSITION SIZING
-------------------
Positions are sized by risk level:

  HIGH risk  =  $2,000 per position (most A/B rated)
  MEDIUM risk = $5,000 per position
  LOW risk   = $10,000 per position

"Risk" here is inversely labelled — LOW risk positions get the largest
size because the edge is most confident. HIGH risk = smaller allocation
because the outcome is more uncertain.

Total deployed: $140,000 across 62 candidates.


6. ENTRY RULES
---------------
A candidate starts in WATCHING state. It does not trade immediately.

Entry trigger: First uptick in price after the edge is identified.
The system watches the contract price and buys on the first positive
price tick. This confirms that the market is beginning to move in the
predicted direction before capital is committed.

In the timeline data, the entry is marked with a "B" (Buy) marker.

Until the first uptick occurs, the candidate remains WATCHING. Some
candidates never trigger and are labelled "Never entered."


7. EXIT RULES
--------------
Positions are exited mechanically. The rules are:

  a) TRAILING STOP — 0.5c from peak (10 occurrences in data)
     If the price retraces 0.5 cents from the highest price reached
     during the trade, the position is closed. Protects profits.

  b) PLATEAU — 4h no improvement (8 occurrences)
     If the price shows no improvement for 4 consecutive hours, exit.
     Prevents capital lockup in dead trades.

  c) MAX HOLD TIME — 12 hours (6 occurrences)
     Hard cap. No position is held longer than 12 hours regardless
     of performance. Forces capital recycling.

  d) TARGET REACHED (3 occurrences)
     If a specific price target is hit, exit with profit.

  e) TOKEN CORRECTION (2 occurrences)
     Minor price correction triggers an exit.


8. KILL RULES
--------------
A position is KILLED (hard stopped) when the thesis breaks:

  a) SENTIMENT REVERSED (8 occurrences)
     The fundamental thesis has reversed. Example: "Direction reversed:
     was lower, now higher." P&L is crystallised at the kill point.

  b) DUPLICATE SLUG RATIONALISATION (6 occurrences)
     The same Polymarket event was tracked under two entries. The
     duplicate is killed; the master is kept. P&L consolidated.

  c) LOTTERY TICKET / SPREAD EATS EDGE (2 occurrences)
     If the entry spread is >= 4c, the edge is consumed by market
     friction. Auto-killed: "4c entry = lottery ticket, spread eats
     edge."

10. MARKET QUALITY FILTERS
----------------------------
The system tracks market quality for each contract:

  Volume:  Ranges from $113 (thin) to $2.0m+ (liquid)
  Spread:  Ranges from 0c (tight) to 3c+ (wide)
  Dead:    Marked with null symbol when no volume

Positions are killed if the spread eats the edge (see kill rule c).
The data includes volume and spread per position in the HTML extract.


11. HOLD DURATION BACKTESTING
-------------------------------
The system tracks "if held longer" scenarios:

  Hold 1h  — +0.6% avg P&L, 29% win rate
  Hold 6h  — +4.3% avg P&L, 54% win rate
  Hold 12h — +8.1% avg P&L, 74% win rate  (current max hold rule)
  Hold 24h — +12.9% avg P&L, 79% win rate
  Hold 48h — +133.4% avg P&L, 83% win rate (only 6 trades reached)

This suggests the 12h hold cap may leave money on the table. The
48h data is from a very small sample (6 trades) and skewed by one
+640% outlier. The 24h data is more statistically meaningful.


12. RE-ENTRY BEHAVIOUR
------------------------
If the system exits a position and the edge later re-emerges, it may
re-enter the same event. In the data:

  - Re-entries get a new signal_id (same event_id)
  - The HTML table shows the same event as separate rows
  - Each row has its own entry price, exit price, and P&L
  - The KILLED-then-re-entered pattern exists (e.g., Tariff on Canada:
    first position killed when direction reversed, later re-entered
    with the correct direction)

14. WHAT THIS DATASET IS FOR
-------------------------------
This audit file exists to:

  - Apply strict mechanical execution rules to historical data
  - Simulate passive bid ladders
  - Model slippage
  - Evaluate missed fills
  - Analyse distribution of price movement after signal
  - Rationalise re-entry behaviour
  - Determine whether the 20% holds are statistically robust or noise
  - Measure how much edge survives after spread/slippage
  - Determine optimal bid ladder spacing from historical volatility
  - Quantify how often re-entries helped vs hurt

Accuracy and structure are more important than performance.
No optimisation has been applied.
